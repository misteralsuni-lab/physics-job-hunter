## 1. TOP INSIGHTS (ranked by impact)

1. **Genuine Pearson Edexcel schools in the Dresden–Berlin–Prague corridor are effectively ONE school (Leonardo V. Academy, Prague).** Pearson's own centre finder lists only THREE German Edexcel centres and none is a mainstream K–12 international school in the corridor; Berlin/Leipzig/Dresden/Prague's major British schools (BBS, BMS, LIS, DIS, PBIS, Park Lane…) all run Cambridge IGCSE + IB, not Edexcel. **Strategic implication:** a strict Edexcel-only stance collapses the live regional target list to a single employer — structurally limiting, not a competition problem. **Resolution:** keep Edexcel as the *primary lane* (preferred, applied first, highest priority) but open a clearly-labeled *secondary "curriculum-adjacent" lane* of Cambridge IGCSE / IB Physics-Maths schools. Per Pearson's own Cambridge↔Edexcel Physics mapping, content overlap is ~90%, and the candidate's Physics/Maths subject expertise transfers directly even when the exam board differs. This multiplies addressable schools from ~1 to ~8+ in-region without abandoning the stated preference ordering.

2. **Physics/Maths demand is real and acute in the region — but it lives in the Cambridge/IB lane.** Berlin British School advertised a full-time Physics teacher (Gr 6–12, "no German required"); Berlin Metropolitan School advertised Maths & Science with Physics specialism; BBIS explicitly welcomes A-Level/IGCSE backgrounds; LVA is actively hiring Maths/Physics. **Implication:** the scarcity is board-specific, not subject-specific — broadening to curriculum-adjacent schools converts a structural dead-end into a realistic, high-demand pipeline.

3. **Visa pathways are more favourable than the board gap suggests.** Germany EU Blue Card applies the lower "bottleneck" STEM threshold (~€45,934) and schools (BBIS, FIS) explicitly sponsor; LVA offers "VISA assistance"; and the Czech 1-July-2024 rule grants free labour-market access to UK/US/CA/AU/NZ/JP/IL/KR/SG nationals (residence permit still needed). **Implication:** confirm candidate nationality early — if on the free-access list, Prague/LVA becomes dramatically easier and the Edexcel-only lane is far more viable.

4. **Competition is inverted between the two lanes.** Edexcel-only: near-zero applicant competition (rare specialism) but near-zero school count → entry difficulty is structural. Curriculum-adjacent: moderate competition but multiple growing schools and documented shortages (German state-system Physics coverage only ~17% of need). **Implication:** the Edexcel lane favours the candidate on merit when a role exists; the adjacent lane favours the candidate on volume.

5. **Cost-of-living geography supports the Dresden base.** Dresden rent is ~48% below Berlin and ~37% below Prague; Leipzig is cheapest overall. All-in cost: Prague ≈ Dresden (Prague goods cheaper, rent higher); Berlin most expensive. **Implication:** commute-based (home-based) targeting of Prague/Berlin schools is financially sound; no forced relocation needed for DE/CZ roles.

6. **A 2–4 week Cambridge IGCSE + IB Physics/Maths familiarisation step multiplies the target set ~8×** (per Pearson mapping, ~90% content overlap) and is the single highest-ROI candidate improvement. **Implication:** treat adjacent-board certification as a near-mandatory parallel investment, not a fallback.

7. **Recruitment timing: open contact Oct–Dec 2026 for Aug 2027 starts; visit Prague on a budget rail trip (€20–40, 2.5h).** International-school peak windows are Jan–Mar (Aug start) and Aug–Sep/Jan (mid-year). Avoid Jul–Aug (schools closed). **Implication:** the pipeline should schedule outreach and a physical-visit flag accordingly, not treat all months equally.

## 5. RISKS & GAPS

1. **Edexcel scarcity (HIGH risk).** Only ~1 genuine Edexcel school exists in the entire Dresden–Berlin–Prague corridor; strict adherence leaves the candidate with a single live employer. *Mitigation:* run the secondary curriculum-adjacent lane (Cambridge/IB) with `adjacency_boost`; invest 2–4 weeks in Cambridge IGCSE + IB Physics/Maths familiarisation (~90% overlap) to multiply addressable schools ~8×.

2. **Visa complexity for non-EU candidate (MEDIUM–HIGH risk).** Germany Blue Card requires ZAB/Anabin qualification recognition; Czech Employee Card requires employer-sponsored vacancy registration. *Mitigation:* target schools with explicit sponsorship (LVA, BBIS, FIS); pursue German Blue Card via the lower STEM bottleneck threshold; confirm candidate nationality against the Czech 1-July-2024 free-access list (UK/US/CA/AU/NZ/JP/IL/KR/SG) — if matched, Prague becomes dramatically easier.

3. **Competition vs. volume inversion (MEDIUM risk).** The Edexcel lane has near-zero competition but near-zero school count (structural entry difficulty); the adjacent lane has volume but moderate competition and selective hiring (e.g., Akademeia Warsaw). *Mitigation:* prioritize Edexcel roles where they exist (LVA, IS OWL) and treat adjacent-lane applications as a volume play with differentiated Edexcel/STEM/engineering positioning.

4. **Timeline / seasonality gap (MEDIUM risk).** International-school hiring peaks Jan–Mar (Aug start) and Aug–Sep/Jan (mid-year); Jul–Aug schools are closed. A mistimed outreach wastes the narrow window. *Mitigation:* schedule pipeline outreach for Oct–Dec 2026 (pre-peak) targeting Aug 2027 starts; flag Jul–Aug as do-not-contact.

5. **Data confidence gaps (MEDIUM risk).** Several Edexcel schools (Akademeia, BIS Warsaw, Avicenna) are rated MEDIUM confidence on facts/careers pages; IS OWL has no public careers URL; candidate nationality and German-language level unconfirmed. *Mitigation:* gate Tier-1 actions on `confidence: high` only; route MEDIUM-confidence schools to Network-First with LinkedIn/TES verification; capture candidate nationality + German level as required profile fields before final ranking.
