3. The "Double Reduction" Policy: A Sectoral Impact
Analysis

The "Double Reduction" policy (Shuang Jian), implemented in July 2021, represents the most draconian
restructuring of the Chinese education market in modern history. Understanding its boundaries is
essential for risk mitigation.

3.1 Policy Mechanics and Intent

The policy was designed to reduce the "twin burdens" of homework and off-campus tutoring on
students in the compulsory education phase (Grades 1–9).¹¹

● Prohibition on Capitalization: Companies teaching core curriculum subjects (Math, English,
Physics) to compulsory education students were barred from raising capital through IPOs and were
forced to register as non-profit organizations.¹³

● Operational Restrictions: Tutoring is strictly forbidden on weekends, national holidays, and
winter/summer breaks.

●  Foreign Teacher Ban: The policy explicitly prohibits the hiring of foreign teachers based 
overseas to
conduct online training for students within China.¹⁴

3.2 The Bifiurcation ofi the Market

The policy has created a stark divide in the employment landscape:


3.2.1 The Collapse ofi the K-9 Training Center Sector

The traditional entry-level market—private language academies (e.g., English First, Disney English)
teaching young children on weekends—has been decimated. For a professional of Mr. Alsuni's caliber,
this sector is now a "no-go" zone. It is fraught with legal risks, instability, and low career 
ceilings.¹³

3.2.2 The Rise ofi the High School and Vocational Sector

Crucially, the Double Reduction policy applies primarily to compulsory education (up to Grade 9). 
Senior
High Schools (Grades 10-12), particularly those offering international curricula (A-Level, IBDP, 
AP),
generally fall outside the strictest punitive measures of the policy.¹³

●  Private Bilingual Schools: These institutions serve Chinese nationals who intend to attend
university abroad. Since these students can no longer attend weekend "cram schools" for Physics
or Math, the burden of exam preparation has shifted entirely to the day school. This has
paradoxically increased the value of high-quality subject teachers within these schools. Parents
expect   the school to deliver all necessary instruction, making experienced A-Level Physics 
teachers
a premium asset.¹⁶

●  Vocational Education: The Chinese government is actively pivoting its educational strategy toward
vocational and technical training. Mr. Alsuni’s engineering background aligns with this national
priority. Roles in vocational colleges or high schools with a "tech" stream are politically safer 
and
encouraged by the state.¹⁷

3.3 Strategic Implication: The "Subject Expert" Pivot

The Double Reduction policy has effectively purged the market of "edutainers"—unqualified foreigners
teaching English based solely on their appearance. The market has matured. Schools are under 
pressure
to demonstrate academic rigor. A non-native Physics teacher with an Engineering degree and four 
years
of results-oriented teaching experience is now arguably more employable in the premium segment than
a native English speaker with a generic History degree and no teaching credentials. The policy has
shifted the metric from "Native Speaker" to "Subject Specialist".¹⁸

4. Immigration Strategy: Z Visa Acquisition fior

Securing the Z Visa (Work Visa) is the gateway to legal employment. For a Libyan national and non-

4.1 The Points-Based System (Category B)

China utilizes a points-based system to classify foreign workers. A score of 60+ points typically 
qualifies
an applicant as Category B.⁴


● Education (Bachelor’s Degree): +10 points.

● Work Experience (2+ Years): +5 to +15 points depending on tenure.

●  Annual Salary: Points are awarded based on the salary offered (e.g., salary over 450k RMB/year
yields high points).

● Age: 26-45 years old is the optimal bracket for points (+15 points).

● HSK (Chinese Language): Optional but adds points.

●  Location: Working in certain zones (e.g., western regions or specific tech parks) can add 
points.

Strategy: Mr. Alsuni should aim to maximize points through salary negotiation and emphasizing his 
age
and experience. However, the most direct route for him is the "Direct Condition" pathway for 
Category
B, which simply requires a Bachelor’s degree + 2 years of relevant experience.⁵

4.2 The "Relevant Degree" Argument

A critical friction point can be the match between the degree and the teaching subject.

●  Challenge: Some local Foreign Expert Bureaus (FEBs) may demand a "Degree in Education" or a
"Degree in Physics" for a Physics teaching role.

●  Solution: The Engineering degree (B.Sc. Engineering Science) is highly compatible. The 
transcript ¹
shows heavy coursework in Mechanics, Thermodynamics, and Fluids. Mr. Alsuni should prepare a
Degree Compatibility Statement, mapping his university modules to the A-Level Physics syllabus,
to be submitted with his visa application if questioned. This demonstrates that he is a "Subject
Expert" by academic training, not just experience.³

4.3 Documentation fior Libyan Citizens

Citizens of certain nations may face longer processing times due to security background checks.

●  Non-Criminal Record Check: This is the most critical document. It must be issued by the relevant
authority in Libya (or current country of residence if resident there for >6 months), notarized
locally,  and then authenticated by the Chinese Embassy in that country.¹⁹ This process can take 1-2
months and should be started immediately, even before a job offer is secured.

●  Degree Authentication: The University of Tripoli degree must also be authenticated by the Chinese
Embassy.²⁰

●  Security Assessment: While Libya is not on a ban list, applicants may be subject to a "security
background check" during the Work Permit Notification phase. This adds roughly 2-4 weeks to the
standard 15-day processing time.²¹

While Mr. Alsuni is a subject teacher, a 120-hour TEFL certificate is strongly recommended.
